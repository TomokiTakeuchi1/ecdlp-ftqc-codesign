- **Independently verified the core physics of the generic Bell-bridge identity** rather than trusting the reported error: `_apply_cnot_q_to_e` plus `h_on_q` is exactly the standard CNOT(control=q, target=e) + H(q) decomposition of a Bell-basis measurement on (q, e); the applied correction `Z^(measured_q) @ X^(measured_e)` on the receiver qubit matches the textbook (Bennett et al.) teleportation correction convention for this specific circuit ordering. I did not need to trust the numeric result alone: `minimum_branch_probability` came out to `0.24999999999999983` -- i.e. essentially exactly 1/4 for every one of 32 Haar-random input states. This is a strong independent confirmation, since the teleportation protocol's defining no-signaling property guarantees each of the four Bell-measurement outcomes occurs with probability exactly 1/4 *regardless of the teleported state*; a flawed circuit (wrong gate, wrong wiring) would generically produce state-dependent, non-uniform branch probabilities, not a value pinned to 1/4 across 32 independently sampled random states. The near-machine-precision Frobenius error (`6.64e-16`) is consistent with this being a correct implementation, not a coincidence.
- Independently re-derived the `6n-4` total-event and `n-1` resource-state-demand formulas from `build_bridge_ripple_trace` by direct counting: `(n-1)` prepares + `n` segment-computes + `2(n-1)` Bell measurements (XX+ZZ per bridge) + `(n-1)` rebinds + `(n-1)` reactions + `1` finalize = `6n-4` exactly; resource-state demand is set to 1 on exactly the first `n-1` segments (all but the last), giving `n-1` total, consistent with `n-1` temporary-AND consumptions. Both match the contract doc's claimed formulas and the code's own validation assertions.
- Traced the causal-order/no-reverse-time-edge claim directly in the dependency-construction code: a receiver segment's `ENTANGLEMENT_READY` dependency is on the *bridge's prepare event*, not on the sender's future output; the destructive `XX`/`ZZ` measurements depend on *both* the prepare event and the sender's completed output (`QUANTUM_ORDER`); the rebind depends on *both* Bell outcomes (`BELL_OUTCOME`) and receiver completion; nothing in the graph has an edge running from a later-indexed event back to an earlier one that isn't already justified by one of these four dependency kinds. The colloquial "teleported backward in time" framing is represented as an ordinary forward-acyclic prepare/use/measure/rebind DAG, matching the doc's explicit claim.
