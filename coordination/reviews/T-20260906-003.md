## Claims requiring independent review

   retains every noncommuting target--control order and every source CNOT
   barrier, while same-layer pairs satisfy the approved joint common-frame
   rule.
2. Because every ordered CCX pair is classified, earliest longest-path ranks
   form a legal schedule and attain the explicit critical-path lower bound;
   macro-depth is exact within this stated logical model.
3. The source/unitary/measured macro-depth table is respectively
   `1/1/1, 2/2/2, 3/3/3, 6/4/3, 6/4/4, 7/5/4, 7/5/5` for widths 2--8;
   each macro contributes exactly two unitless direct-CCZ candidate levels.
4. Measurement cleanup preserves complete clean OOP arithmetic and relative
   phase at every width 2--8, with explicit lifetime, release, suffix, and
   outcome-frame propagation. Its depth benefit is non-monotone: widths 5 and
   7 improve, while 6 and 8 do not.
5. Reaction frontiers are deliberately conservative `CHECK` records retaining
   six outcome tokens per predecessor event. Their minimal subset and duration
   are unresolved, and no finite-backend quantity is emitted.

## Findings

shipped replay module, only the already-reviewed `build_oop_qcla_trace`).

1. **Pairwise dependency reconstruction at every width.** Reimplemented the
   dependency rule (edge iff noncommuting target/control overlap OR a source
   CNOT layer lies strictly between the two source layers) and the
   longest-path ASAP layering from scratch, run against the real
   `build_oop_qcla_trace(width)` for every width 2-8 and both variants
   (unitary and measured-cleanup, the latter with `PREFIX_UNCOMPUTE` Toffolis
   removed first). For every one of the 776 real pairs, cross-checked the
   dependency-rule prediction against an independent brute-force truth-table
   commutation check (`assert commutes(gi,gj) == (not noncommuting)`) -- zero
   contradictions. Result table (source/unitary/measured CCX count and
   macro-depth) for widths 2-8:
   `1/1/1, 3/2×2/2, 5/3×2/3, 10/4/9→3, 12/4/11→4, 17/5/15→4, 19/5/17→5`

   session's CCX/cleanup counts, for every width and both variants.
2. **Critical-path optimality argument.** Verified the mathematics directly:
   the recurrence `depth(g) = 1 + max(depth(p) : p -> g)` is the standard
   longest-path-ending-here DP over a DAG processed in source order (valid
   since every constructed edge goes from an earlier to a later source
   index); a dependency edge between two operations forces
   `depth(successor) > depth(predecessor)`, so no two operations placed at
   the same depth can have a direct edge; and this remains true even when a
   chain of edges (not a direct edge) forces separation, since the DP
   propagates transitively through intermediate depths regardless of whether
   the pairwise relation itself is transitively closed. This is exactly the
   textbook "unlimited-width list scheduling achieves the critical-path
   lower bound" result, so the "PROVED optimal within this stated logical
   model" claim is mathematically sound, not merely empirically observed.
   I found no missing ordering class: the two conditions (noncommuting
   target/control, source-Clifford barrier) are checked for literally every
   ordered pair, so no case is silently skipped.
3. **Aggregate independent-replay counts.** Recomputed from first principles
   (no import of either scheduler/replay module):

     over widths 2-8 = **776**, matching exactly.
   - Truth-table rows: `sum(2^|union support|)` over all 776 pairs =
     **39,824**, matching exactly.
   - Arithmetic inputs: `sum(4^width) * 2 variants` over widths 2-8 =
     16+64+256+1024+4096+16384+65536, doubled = **174,752**, matching
     exactly.
   - Measured branch columns: `sum(4^width * 2^cleanups)` for widths 5-8
     (2048+8192+65536+262144) = **337,920**, matching exactly.
   - Phase-sensitive relative pairs: `sum(2^cleanups * (4^width - 1))` for
     widths 5-8 (2046+8190+65532+262140) = **337,908**, matching exactly.
   All five totals match to the exact integer, independently confirming the
   scale of the exhaustive replay is real and not a fabricated headline.
